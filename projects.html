Technical Method

How the work is actually executed

The recurring engineering tasks that sit inside every conversion and commissioning programme we run.

Route survey & utility detection

Before excavation the route is walked and scanned with electromagnetic utility locators to map existing water, gas, telecom and power services. Detected services are marked, and where a conflict exists the alignment is revised or the crossing redesigned as a trenchless bore.

Trenching, bedding & ducting

Trenches are cut to the depth and width specified for the voltage class, bedded with screened sand, and cables laid with correct spacing and bending radius. Protective covers and warning tape go in before backfill, which is compacted in layers to prevent later settlement.

HDD & trenchless crossings

Carriageways, drains and rail-side land are crossed by horizontal directional drilling with our own rigs — a pilot bore is steered to the exit pit, reamed to size, and the duct bundle pulled through, avoiding road cutting and the reinstatement liability that follows it.

RMU & DTR foundations

Plinths are cast to utility-approved drawings with cable entry provision, duct sleeves and earthing pits set into the pour. Level and orientation are verified before switchgear or transformer placement, because a foundation error becomes a termination problem later.

Jointing & termination

HT straight-through joints and terminations are made by qualified jointers using manufacturer-approved kits under controlled cleanliness. Every joint is recorded with location, kit batch and jointer identity, so a future fault can be traced back to a known point.

Testing & energisation

Before charging, cables undergo insulation resistance and high-voltage withstand testing, earthing systems are measured, and protection relay settings are verified against the coordination study. Energisation proceeds only on a cleared permit with signed-off test records.
